## What a puff number is

A puff rating on a sealed disposable is a manufacturer claim. It changes with puff length and how the person draws. "Up to 9000" is not a promise that every unit delivers 9000 puffs. When a comparison table shows one puff number for a whole brand, it is usually one SKU, or a range that got flattened. Keep the model name next to the number.

## Ranges, not a winner's trophy

The same desk pass put publicly listed sealed disposables commonly marketed to adult Australians in a wide band: about 1,900 puffs at the familiar compact end, up to the Ingot 9000's 9,000, with some rechargeable names advertising 10,000 or more. Those rechargeable names are a different class. Do not paste them into the sealed-disposable row.

Tank size in that pass: many familiar compact models sit around 2-12 ml. The Ingot 9000 lists 22 ml.

A dual LED (battery and e-liquid) was still uncommon among the competitor models tracked in the note. On a unit sold as an Ingot 9000, a missing LED is treated there as an authenticity warning, not as a normal spec variant of that catalogue.

Brand familiarity can still matter more than the largest tank. A capacity table does not choose a flavour.

## How the Ingot 9000 LED is read

Two readings, not one.

- E-liquid empty: the device is finished, even if the battery indicator still shows charge.
- Battery empty and vapour falling away: finished.
- A flash with little or no vapour, a burnt taste, or vapour that thins suddenly: usual end-of-life signs.
- Do not open the case and do not try to charge this model. It has no charge port.

Exact flash patterns belong to the device in hand. This note does not publish a blink code.

## Disposal checklist (Australia)

A finished sealed disposable still contains a lithium cell.

- Do not put it in household rubbish or the kerbside recycling bin.
- Hand it in whole at an e-waste or battery drop-off. Ask the local council which site accepts it. Do not open it to pull the cell out.
- Swelling, unusual heat, a burning or chemical smell, leaking liquid, hissing or smoke: stop using it.
- If it is hot, and it is safe to move, set it on concrete, tile or a metal tray away from anything that can burn. Do not put it in water.
- If it is smoking or on fire, keep clear and call 000.
- Liquid on skin: wash with soap and water. If someone may have swallowed liquid, call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26.
- Tell drop-off staff when a unit is swollen, leaking or damaged.
- Airlines generally want lithium devices in cabin baggage, not checked bags. Check that airline before flying.
- Store spares cool and dry, not in a hot car, and away from children, pets, keys and coins.
